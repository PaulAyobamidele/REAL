## 3. Domain assumptions — the third lever (D) now works  (Notes §8.12-8.18)

Rounds 1-2 stated **no** assumptions about the world, so every failure was
blamed on the car by default. Now:

- The requirement has both slots: `assuming` (D) and `ensuring` (soft goals,
  R) — `docs/examples/R_baseline.dsl`.
- After every simulation each assumption is **held, broken, or not
  measured**; a failure is spurious only if one was broken.
- Each assumption gets a verdict: load-bearing, not load-bearing, untested…
- **Assumptions about the car are refused** (otherwise failures could be
  defined away).
- In the review, assumptions can be kept, tightened, loosened, dropped or
  added; the tool writes them into the next requirement as [D] changes.

Result: re-judged under the baseline assumptions, **every failure in rounds
1-2 stays the car's fault**, and the rounds give no evidence about any
assumption (none was broken in real encounters).

## 4. Our own test scene was flawed — and we fixed it  (Notes §8.19-8.38)

Reading the scene closely, then five short test runs on Narval, exposed one
flaw at a time:

| Test run | What it caught | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| reading the code | pedestrian waited until the car was 8 m away; "right-to-left" never crossed; the distance setting did nothing; ~25 % of runs the car drove away | new scene v2 |
| smoke 1 | the car drove over the kerb | car placed along its own lane |
| smoke 2 | a Scenic error (crashed in 4 min) | a **laptop check** that builds the real scenes in 6 s before any Narval run |
| smoke 3 | the car stayed on the road ✓, but **the pedestrian walked away from the road** | pedestrian steered to the far kerb by position; every run records whether it crossed |
| smoke 4 | crossing works ✓, but too early — road empty when the car arrived | pedestrian steps out to **meet the car**, with a 6 s timeout |
| smoke 5 | **all mechanics work** ✓ | — |

Consequence for the paper: round 1's "left-to-right crossings fail 24 points
more often" was at least partly a **scene artefact**. REAL has a label for
this kind of finding: [T], the test itself was wrong.

**First trustworthy lead about the car** (smoke 5, one trial each):
proportional braking stops for *any* person it sees — 9-13 m short while
the pedestrian is still on the kerb — and in 3 of 4 runs never drives on
while the pedestrian waits on the far kerb. Round 2's standoff, now measured
on a valid scene.

## 5. Measuring the promise (R)  (Notes §8.20)

Every simulation now records pedestrian speed, braking smoothness
(deceleration, jerk), time-to-collision, and whether and when the car moved
on. Soft goals such as `resume_within_s <= 10` are checked per run (met /
missed). Test defects (car off the road, pedestrian never crossed, scenario
not simulated) are set aside and named, never counted as passes.

## 6. Smart search (GE) is the main search now  (Notes §8.22-8.24)

- A new grammar with **21,120 scenarios** (fog 0-100, car distance 10-50 m,
  walking speed 1-4 m/s, crossing trigger) — deliberately beyond the
  assumptions, so we learn whether they matter.
- GE runs through the same path as the grid on Narval.
- Reports on GE runs say that GE samples unevenly, count each scenario once,
  and mark findings as "leads"; a small **grid check** re-runs GE's best and
  worst scenarios fairly.
